# Design notes

## M5-A — offline trajectory evaluation contract and interpretation

- `trajectory_evaluation.py` is a separate reader of frozen M4 exports and KITTI truth. Online coverage and scoreable poses come exclusively from `per_frame.jsonl`. `estimated_poses.json` is a mixed pose-stage export and must not be combined with online reports. `final_keyframes.json` may prove that an initialization anchor is the local identity pose, but its anchor-only pose does not count as online tracking. The baseline exports no final keyframes, so anchor-reference orientation there follows the saved M2/M3 initialization convention.
- The existing `KittiGraySequence` indexes sorted PNGs by position, while the evaluator indexes KITTI truth by original six-digit image stem. For the fixed source prefix, stems, sorted positions, timestamp rows, online `frame_id`, and GT rows agree at 0–299. Offline validation rejects missing or renumbered image stems, malformed timestamps, frame-ID gaps, out-of-range truth, and inconsistent `T_cw` centers. This does not revise the online loader; a later nonprefix extraction would need a separately reviewed loader/source-ID design. KITTI pose rows do not carry independent timestamps, so agreement with an external timing source is not independently provable.
- `T_cw` means `X_c=R_cw X_w+t_cw`; `C_w=-R_cw.T t_cw` and `R_wc=R_cw.T`. KITTI gives `T_wc`. Each independent segment fits proper positive-scale Umeyama `C_gt=sQ C_est+b`. A segment requires at least five valid poses, finite/nonrepeated centers, and `sigma2/sigma1 >= 0.05` in both centered center clouds; `sigma3` may be zero. This predeclared rule avoids unstable 3D rotation about an almost straight path. The segment-specific fit is in-sample, so its ATE can be optimistic. Relative errors retain exact source-frame gaps and do not bridge losses or separately initialized maps.
- Relative translation vectors are `R_cw_i(C_j-C_i)` for the estimate and `R_wc_gt_i.T(C_gt_j-C_gt_i)` for truth, both in the first camera's axes. Direction is reported only when the estimated vector is nonzero and the GT displacement is at least 0.05 m. A single segment scale converts estimated lengths to meters for displacement and relative vector errors; `s||d_est||/||d_gt||` is the displacement ratio. Its first-versus-second-half median for exact-gap-5 pairs is a short-window drift diagnostic, not a global scale estimate. Aligned orientation uses `Q R_wc_est`; separate anchor-referenced orientation uses the GT orientation at the map origin without adding the anchor to online coverage.
- Own-eligible and common-frame ablation metrics answer different questions. Own-eligible counts are 13/97, 47/126, 58/145, and 58/145 online poses. The matched comparison uses 12 eligible frames, 98–109, after requiring segment fits on the same common source IDs in all variants. The descriptor-maintenance and full-M4 published online trajectories are identical because corrected full M4 accepted no BA updates. Apparent differences among own-eligible errors also reflect different scoreable frames and independent segment fits; they do not by themselves prove one feature caused an accuracy improvement.
- Full-M4 segment 4 has a prefix-fit/held-out-suffix RMSE of 0.6149 m compared with its optimistic full-segment in-sample RMSE of 0.2250 m. Segment 5's prefix is ill conditioned and has no out-of-sample translation score. The fitted segment scales, 2.498513 and 8.195958 m per local unit, differ because each initialization defines an arbitrary unit baseline; their difference is not within-segment drift. The small early/late displacement-ratio changes over 29 and 19 gap-5 pairs cannot establish robust long-run scale control.
- The first M4.5 task, if authorized, should correct the confirmed BA negative-depth penalty residual that adds one scalar to both pixel coordinates. A targeted test should show that behind-camera observations cannot cancel the penalty through pixel residual signs; finite-difference Jacobian, fixed gauge, rollback, and outside-window safeguards must remain intact. Compare accepted updates, held-out reprojection, online poses, and runtime against frozen M4, without weakening gates. Next, diagnose each PnP loss at association, solver, and validation stages: failures at 43 and 207 have only 6 and 10 correspondences, while 82 and 282 have 40/83 and 31/70 inliers but fail the current fraction gate. These are evidence of fragmentation, not proof that the gate is wrong. Long initialization gaps after 122 and 207 warrant candidate-overlap and motion diagnostics; 38/56 rejected attempts cite low parallax, a justified geometric safeguard until contrary evidence exists. No new thresholds follow from M5-A alone.
- Full M5-B remains separate: restarted 0–149 and 150–299 tracker runs, native-inclusive process RSS if feasible, and broader robustness/demonstration work. The corrected M4 source artifacts remain frozen.

## M2 — fully accepted resampling trials

- Match-order trials yield two distinct diagnostics: a provisional rotation may exist even when the one-shot initialization rejects, whereas a fully accepted trial passes every geometry and map-quality gate. `pose_stability_valid_candidates` includes both kinds of rotation for the angular statistic; `pose_stability_provisional_candidates` counts rejected trials with a rotation; `pose_stability_accepted_trials` counts full successes. The current acceptance rule requires at least 4 candidate rotations, median rotation disagreement ≤10°, and at least 4 fully accepted trials among the eight fixed permutations. Provisional rotations never satisfy the accepted-trial minimum. An isolated failure of that minimum returns `insufficient_accepted_pose_stability_trials` and clears public pose/map fields while keeping diagnostic evidence. Setting trials to zero bypasses the check.
- The corrected gate keeps all 12 fixed KITTI decisions, with accepted trials 8/8, 7/8, 7/8, and 7/8 on the four accepted original pairs and 8/8 on the accepted held-out pair. The 20→25 anomaly remains rejected for 42.36° disagreement, with only 1/8 trials fully accepted. Synthetic weak-baseline acceptance becomes 0/5 rather than 2/5. Most rejected trials fail cheirality support or minimum pose-inlier count. Thus a stable provisional orientation does not imply a stable fully reconstructible map. ## M1 — Initial design choices

1. **Independent project.** The supplied GitHub repository is ROS/catkin material, while this project targets inspectable Python/OpenCV. No source was transplanted. The local task directory was empty and not a Git checkout when inspected.
2. **Narrow image abstraction.** `ImageSample`, `ImageSequence`, `KittiGraySequence`, and `SyntheticSequence` are enough for two-image processing. `Frame`, `Keyframe`, `MapPoint`, and `Map` wait until observation identities, poses, and ownership rules are clear.
3. **Camera convention.** `CalibratedCamera` stores a zero-skew pinhole matrix in pixels. KITTI odometry `P0` supplies the left rectified grayscale camera intrinsic block; `image_0` determines image dimensions. This milestone assumes rectified images and does not handle radial distortion or other camera models.
4. **No pose leakage.** The loader reads images, `P0`, and optional timestamps only. It has no ground-truth pose path or scale input.
5. **ORB and matching.** OpenCV ORB provides keypoints and 256-bit descriptors. Brute-force Hamming KNN provides two candidates; Lowe-style 0.75 ratio filtering removes ambiguous neighbors. A reverse nearest-neighbor consistency check removes duplicate/nonmutual assignments. These are descriptor filters, not geometric verification. Alternatives considered: pure cross-check (simpler but no ambiguity ratio) and optical flow (useful later, but not this ORB descriptor milestone).
6. **Diagnostics as a stable boundary.** Stage counts are written to JSON. Unknown future fields are `null`; tracking is `not_estimated`. This prevents the current match count from being mistaken for essential-matrix/PnP inliers.
7. **Synthetic fallback.** Fixed-seed grayscale texture plus a fixed image translation gives a repeatable demonstration and tests. It is not a 3D scene, an initialization test, or a scale benchmark.
8. **Configuration and logging.** `configs/default.json` contains ORB, match, and drawing settings. The CLI uses Python logging for progress and JSON for persistent measurements. No GUI is required.

## Decisions identified after M1 (pose and initialization items resolved in M2 below)

- Define camera-to-world versus world-to-camera transforms and frame-index/observation conventions before geometry code.
- Decide initialization parallax, cheirality, and reprojection gates using synthetic geometry tests, then a fixed KITTI development slice.
- Fix gauge freedoms explicitly in sliding-window BA. Keep the active window near 5–10 keyframes, use `least_squares` with sparse Jacobian structure, and measure the tradeoff between accuracy and runtime.
- Separate online estimation from offline evaluation modules. An Umeyama Sim(3) alignment may be used for ATE only; scale drift remains a reported limitation.
- Loop closure is optional after the core system and failure analysis work.

## M2 coordinate and scale convention (established before geometry implementation)

- `T_cw` maps world coordinates into camera coordinates: `X_c = R_cw X_w + t_cw`. The first camera defines the arbitrary world frame, so `R1 = I` and `t1 = 0`.
- The recovered second pose is `T_c2w = (R_21, t_21)`, where `X_c2 = R_21 X_c1 + t_21`. Its center in first-camera/world coordinates is `C2 = -R_21.T @ t_21`.
- Pixel-coordinate projection and triangulation use `P1 = K[I|0]`, `P2 = K[R_21|t_21]`. A normalized ray is `normalize(K^-1 [u,v,1])`; the second ray is rotated into the first frame by `R_21.T` before parallax is measured. Intrinsics are applied exactly once.
- `recoverPose` supplies only translation direction. Normalize `t_21` to unit length and use one arbitrary baseline unit for triangulation. No KITTI pose, synthetic depth, or other ground truth sets online scale. Synthetic truth is used only in tests/offline reports.
- Preserve original match-list indices through essential RANSAC, pose recovery, and triangulation. The RANSAC mask remains separate from the cheirality/pose mask and final accepted-point mask.

## M2 calibrated geometry and acceptance decisions

- `findEssentialMat` receives **pixel** correspondences and `K` once, with RANSAC confidence 0.999 and threshold 1.5 **pixels**. Its original mask is preserved. OpenCV may return vertically or horizontally stacked essential candidates; reject malformed/nonfinite matrices and choose the valid candidate with the most pose-recovery inliers. `recoverPose` runs with the RANSAC mask and also receives pixel coordinates plus `K`. Its cheirality mask is recorded separately.
- Initialization requires at least 8 inputs to attempt a five-point RANSAC solution, 40 essential inliers and 40% inlier ratio, 30 pose inliers, and 25 accepted points. These minimum counts avoid initializing from a tiny coincidental consensus. Lower counts reject explicitly; they can be tuned on a fixed development set, not per frame.
- Triangulation uses pixel projection matrices `K[I|0]` and `K[R_21|t_21]`. Filters retain finite homogeneous points with nonzero `w`, positive depth in both cameras, depth at most 200 **unit baselines**, ray parallax at least 0.5° per point and 1.0° median, and reprojection error at most 3.0 **pixels in each image**. The upper depth bound guards unstable far points; the parallax bound rejects small-baseline geometry even when pixel displacement comes from rotation. Camera-2 rays are rotated into camera-1 coordinates before the ray angle is calculated.
- A homography is a conservative degeneracy diagnostic, not a replacement for the essential matrix. It rejects only if its RANSAC consensus covers at least 90% of inputs, exceeds the essential consensus by at least 5%, **and** candidate median parallax is below 2°. This catches a noiseless pure-rotation case that can otherwise yield a spurious translation; it may also reject genuinely planar, low-parallax initialization, which is preferable to an unstable sparse map. Planar scenes with sufficient parallax are not rejected merely for homography support.
- The synthetic benchmark contains 450 visible noncoplanar landmarks at depths 4–12 scene units, baseline 0.8 scene units, nominal rotation 3°, Gaussian pixel noise 0.35, seed 7. Truth generates observations and evaluates results afterward; the estimator receives only 2D coordinates and calibration. Seed 7 produced 1.246° rotation error, 1.842° translation-direction error, 427 essential/pose inliers, 427 accepted points, 4.039° median parallax, and 0.261/0.261 px median reprojection errors. Pose bias can create sizable raw 3D depth error; an offline single-scale alignment gives 0.565 scene-unit median 3D error. That alignment is evaluation only and never changes the estimated PLY/map.
- An image-based test renders independent textured landmarks at valid projected positions. M1 ORB plus descriptor filtering retained 348 matches; geometry accepted 260 points with 0.165°/0.686° rotation/direction error. Rendered sprites are a controlled software test, not natural-image realism. The original M1 translated-image fallback remains only a matching test. Pure rotation, low baseline, and heavy correspondence outliers reject under the current gates. The later genuine KITTI measurements are recorded below.
- `landmarks.ply` contains 3D points in camera-1 world coordinates, with unknown scale; `poses.json` contains both `T_cw` matrices and world-frame camera centers. The PNG map is an oblique 2D projection of those 3D coordinates. These exports are interoperable with a future optional Blender script; no Blender or GPU package enters the estimator. Optional CUDA work, if ever justified, should use an isolated environment and verify actual GPU access first; `nvidia-smi`'s CUDA compatibility is not a Toolkit version.

## M3 — Map identity, tracking conventions, and limits

- `Frame` stores source index, timestamp, extracted ORB features, optional accepted `T_cw`, state, failure reason, and feature-to-point associations. `MapPoint` stores finite world-frame coordinates, one anchor-frame uint8 ORB descriptor, and unique `{frame_id: keypoint_index}` observations. `SparseMap` owns frames and points for one segment and validates reciprocal references. Point IDs increase globally across new segments; segment IDs distinguish independent origins and scales. A frame feature is assigned to at most one point, and a point is observed at most once per frame.
- `initialize_two_view()` remains the only initial geometry gate. For each accepted-mask position, its original `DMatch` supplies first-view `queryIdx` and second-view `trainIdx`; `points_xyz` is consumed in the order of accepted mask indices. No coordinate-nearest-neighbor association or rejected/provisional point reaches the map. The first anchor has internal identity `T_cw`; its earlier timeline record remains `initializing` with a null pose. The second accepted view carries `R_21,t_21` and begins `tracking`.
- Default search tries gaps 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 from a retained anchor. If no attempt passes, the last candidate becomes a new anchor. There is no special KITTI frame list. Tracking failure marks only the current frame `lost` with null pose, records the prior successful frame separately, and starts a bounded 60-frame fresh-initialization search on the next frame. New segments are not joined geometrically. If recovery exhausts the window, frames remain lost without a fabricated pose.
- Map matching uses the existing ORB Hamming/ratio/mutual matcher against a fixed set of anchor descriptors. A unique descriptor match yields one 3D world point and one current pixel feature. `solvePnPRansac` uses EPNP with 3 px threshold, 0.999 confidence, 100 iterations; inlier-only iterative refinement then requires at least 12 correspondences, 12 inliers, 0.5 inlier fraction, 0.9 positive-depth fraction, and median/p90 inlier reprojection ≤2.5/4 px. RANSAC and reprojection limits are in pixels; depth is a fraction. `T_cw` maps map-world points into the current camera, and `C_w=-R_cw.T@t_cw`. Only accepted PnP inliers add observations. No new landmarks are triangulated during tracking.
- Physical rendered-image tests include accepted 0→3 initialization, multiple PnP poses, a rejected 0→1 attempt followed by success, pure rotation and tiny baseline rejection, blank-frame loss, and fresh independent 8→11 initialization. Synthetic truth is used only after tracking to check errors and is never passed to the estimator. The KITTI run yields 84 PnP-tracked frames but only 2–13 valid poses per segment; 12 losses are caused by insufficient associations or inliers. The single anchor descriptor and fixed landmark set are likely contributors, while M2's 71 rejected attempts account for long initializing spans. Pose accuracy on those M3 KITTI segments is unmeasured; neither low inlier reprojection nor successful PnP alone proves a correct global path.
- JSON exports use `T_cw`, `C_w`, frame and segment IDs, and null invalid poses. PLY coordinates are segment-local in arbitrary monocular units. Blender may import each segment separately; any connection between segments requires separately estimated alignment. No CUDA or Blender library enters the runtime.

## M3 — Recovery-exhaustion policy and diagnostic populations

- Root cause: after failed PnP, `process()` moved LOST to INITIALIZING on every later source ID, while `_initialize()` compared that ID with an unchanged `recovery_start`. Once expired, each frame immediately moved back to LOST, retained an obsolete anchor, and ran no useful search. A three-frame-budget reproduction lost tracking at 7, searched on 8–10, first timed out at 11, then repeated the same timeout on every later frame.
- `max_recovery_frames` now counts **processed calls after the failed PnP frame**, not differences between source indices or elapsed time. A budget of three permits frames 8, 9, and 10 to participate in the recovery search after failure at 7. Frame 11 enters terminal LOST if no map has initialized. The first timeout records `recovery_window_exhausted`; subsequent frames record `terminal_recovery_exhausted` with null pose, no active map, no stale anchor/image, no ORB extraction, and no internal LOST → INITIALIZING → LOST cycle. Their last successful frame and failed segment appear only as diagnostics. A new `MonocularTracker` explicitly starts a separate run. Nonconsecutive source IDs consume one budget unit each when processed; skipped IDs consume none.
- A rejected PnP frame is not inserted into the old map, contributes no observations, and leaves earlier accepted poses, point coordinates, and anchor descriptors unchanged. The old segment remains frozen; a successful recovery before the deadline receives a distinct map segment and globally distinct landmark IDs. Unit tests check the final permitted frame, first and later terminal frames, immutable map data, separate restart, and nonconsecutive IDs. A separate physical synthetic timeout run under `artifacts/m3-synthetic-recovery-timeout/` yields one timeout at frame 11 after a blank-frame failure at 7.
- `per_frame.csv` and JSONL distinguish descriptor candidate/ratio/unique association counts, PnP input correspondences, whether RANSAC ran, returned inliers, accepted/rejected status and reason, map-point count, and recovery state. `recovery_events.json` records timeout events. Summary correspondence distributions include all tracking attempts, with separate accepted and rejected subsets. Inlier distributions include only RANSAC returns with an inlier count, again separated into accepted and rejected subsets; failed calls without a returned count are excluded rather than represented by zero.
- The corrected KITTI sequence-00 0–299 run preserves every original state, failure reason, segment ID, valid pose, accepted pair, and map-point count. The 12 PnP failures remain five insufficient-correspondence and seven insufficient-inlier cases. There were no 60-frame recovery timeouts. The fixed initial map and single anchor descriptor still limit segment length; M4 map growth and keyframe maintenance may address that limitation, but accepted PnP support alone does not establish trajectory accuracy.

## M4 — Map ownership and local optimization decisions

- `SparseMap` owns frames, persistent `Keyframe` references, map points, and one segment ID. Keyframes refer to valid tracked `Frame` objects; the two initialization views become the first keyframes without retroactively publishing an anchor-frame online pose. The active window keeps both initialization views as fixed gauge anchors and up to six recent views, while historical keyframes/observations remain. A map point keeps exact `{frame ID: integer keypoint index}` observations and reciprocal frame associations. Inactive points retain those references but are omitted from PnP.
- Insertion waits at least five frames, then uses estimated camera displacement (≥0.5 arbitrary units), tracked coverage (<0.6 of the previous keyframe associations), or a ten-frame maximum gap. Neither KITTI indices nor true motion decide insertion. At most 80 new points per insertion limit growth. New points come from unassigned ORB descriptor matches between two accepted keyframes and triangulation with `P_i=K[R_i|t_i]`, `P_j=K[R_j|t_j]`; no baseline renormalization is performed. The per-pair estimated center distance sets only relative depth and duplicate thresholds, not metric scale. Matching rejects assigned keypoints; geometry checks both depths, viewing-ray parallax in one world frame, finite homogeneous coordinates, reprojection, and nearby existing 3D points.
- A landmark's first accepted descriptor starts its evidence. Each accepted PnP observation may add one observed uint8 ORB descriptor, retaining the latest eight. The representative is the evidence descriptor with minimum summed pairwise Hamming distance; ties choose the earliest retained entry. Binary descriptors are never averaged. A rejected PnP contributes no descriptor or map observation. A newly triangulated point may include its two accepted keyframe descriptors. A vectorized XOR/bit-count implementation replaced an equivalent Python pairwise norm loop after the development smoke exposed a large CPU cost; fixed KITTI decisions did not change.
- `cull_inconsistent_points` checks recently observed points once they have at least three observations and deactivates points with median reprojection above 6 px. A stale-point cap may deactivate old landmarks if a segment exceeds 1,000 active points and a candidate has not been seen for at least 25 source-frame indices. Historical point identities and observations are retained. No points were culled in the fixed KITTI ablation, so this policy's impact on real tracking remains unmeasured. A failed map-growth candidate rolls back new point IDs and feature associations without invalidating an already accepted PnP pose.
- Local BA uses the actual keyframe–landmark observation graph. Each residual is the two-pixel difference between `project(K,R_cw,t_cw,X_w)` and its stored keypoint. Rodrigues vectors parameterize variable rotations. The first initialization `T_cw=[I|0]` and second initialization pose remain fixed: this removes global SE(3) gauge and preserves the original unit baseline/scale. The optimizer requires at least three window keyframes, 12 multiview points, more scalar residuals than variables, and connection of variable views to the fixed anchors. Caps are eight active keyframes, 60 optimized points, 300 observations, and 12 evaluations. SciPy `least_squares` uses a two-row-per-observation sparse Jacobian pattern and soft-L1 loss with 2 px scale. No GT or fake measurements enter the objective.
- BA builds a candidate parameter vector and applies it only after finite rotations/points, positive depths, reduced squared residual sum, bounded median training reprojection, and no >0.5 px held-out median regression. The current code reports solver status and evaluation count even when a bounded run reaches its evaluation cap; passing geometric/quality gates, rather than the solver's convergence flag alone, determines application. Accepted updates refresh keyframe poses and point coordinates; failed windows do not mutate the map. `per_frame.jsonl` records online published results; M4 `estimated_poses.json` labels final revised keyframe poses separately. Segment-local PLY uses the final point positions.
- The fixed four-way KITTI comparison uses unchanged M2 and PnP thresholds. Growth improves valid poses 97→126; adding descriptor evidence reaches 145; full BA reaches 144 but extends the longest valid span 13→60 and reduces PnP losses 12→6. The full configuration accepts 4/10 BA windows; six training-cost improvements are rejected because held-out reprojection worsens. This is a reason to keep the held-out gate, not to loosen it from KITTI outcomes. The full run has 2,560 valid points across historical segments, 368 in its final active segment, and no culled points. No real KITTI trajectory accuracy inference follows from PnP support or local reprojection statistics.

## M4 — Targeted BA validation and capacity correction

- The earlier held-out filter limited cameras to those in the optimization pose dictionary. It missed any stored observation outside that window when an optimized landmark moved. The corrected affected set is every untrained observation whose landmark or camera pose changes. Measurements are actual stored keypoints; candidate projection uses the proposed point/camera only when that state changes, otherwise the original segment-local state. This includes non-keyframes, historical keyframes, fixed boundary cameras, and observations of fixed landmarks seen by a moving camera. Active and inactive stored points are included when their camera changes; only eligible active points are optimized. Training and validation identities never overlap.
- Every candidate needs at least ten validation measurements. Report total/outside-window counts and before/after median, p90, and maximum pixel error. Reject nonfinite/nonpositive-depth validation, any newly >4 px measurement, median growth >0.5 px, p90 growth >1 px, or maximum growth >2 px. These thresholds are fixed in `configs/default.json`, not tuned per KITTI window. The controlled 30-point regression has near-zero original outside error and >3 px median after a training-improving candidate; the transaction leaves every pose, point, descriptor, and reciprocal identity unchanged. A second controlled budget-terminated candidate with improving external geometry applies.
- SciPy `success=true` means convergence/termination accepted by its solver criteria; `status=0` and `success=false` mean the configured `max_nfev` budget stopped iteration, while a negative status is a solver failure. `nfev` counts function evaluations. A budget-terminated point is not called converged: it may apply only after the same full geometric, training, and external validation gates. Empty/too-small validation populations reject. All four originally applied KITTI windows reported `success=false,status=0,nfev=12`; in the corrected fixed run all ten windows are budget-terminated and rejected by external regression, despite training improvement. This is a conservative result rather than a search for higher acceptance.
- The active-point cap is hard per segment. Before new triangulated proposals are admitted, an already overfull map removes stale points first, then weaker/older points deterministically. Only remaining slots accept proposals in match order with original keypoint indices; excess proposals never acquire IDs. Inactive point objects and observations remain for audit and reciprocal integrity. Memory across historical segments is not globally bounded. The BA optimization block remains capped at eight keyframes, 60 points, 300 training observations, and 12 evaluations; complete affected-observation validation can grow with retained map history. Both fixed initialization poses stay fixed, removing gauge but retaining their estimation bias. A graph must connect the two anchors after point/observation caps.
- Online `per_frame.jsonl` is immutable chronological output. `estimated_poses.json` revises online-valid keyframes but keeps the originally unestimated anchor null in its chronological list. `final_keyframes.json` contains every final adjusted keyframe pose, including that anchor. Final-map PNGs use the same final keyframe objects with final landmark positions; a separate baseline view retains online trajectory poses. PLY contains final segment-local landmarks and arbitrary monocular scale. Neither rejected BA variables nor cross-segment links enter these outputs.
- The original M4 ablations remain preserved. Corrected synthetic varied visibility keeps 12/13/15/15 valid poses, but full BA rejects its one candidate after 115 validation measurements newly exceed 4 px. Corrected KITTI keeps baseline/growth/descriptor trajectories identical; full now has 145 valid poses and longest span 34, with 0/10 BA applied, versus the original 144 and 60 with 4/10 applied. M4 is accepted for its defined functional and safety criteria. No real-data BA benefit is demonstrated. KITTI trajectory accuracy and drift remain unscored, process peak RSS is unmeasured, and independent map segments retain unrelated arbitrary scales. M5 has not started.
